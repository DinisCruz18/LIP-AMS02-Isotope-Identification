# AMS-02 Isotopic Separation: Simulation Plots & Diagnostics

This directory contains the visual diagnostics and final results of the Monte Carlo simulation for cosmic Boron isotopes ($^{10}B$ and $^{11}B$) interacting with the AMS-02 detector. 

All current diagnostics are generated via the Python analytical pipeline. The kinematic sanity checks and instrumental resolution plots are produced directly by `ams_basic_plots.py` and `ams_advanced_plots.py`. The analytical templates and statistical fitting plots are generated by the core project scripts (`ams_mass_templates.py` and `ams_fraction_fitter.py`), which are located in the root repository directory as they serve as the fundamental physics engine for this analysis. The `.root` data files containing the simulated trees and templates required to reproduce all visual results are also hosted within this repository directory.

**Production Run Parameters**
The plots in this directory were generated using the following locked hyperparameter configuration:
* **Total Events:** 1,000,000 
* **Isotopic Abundance:** 30% $^{10}B$ 
* **Tracker Resolution:** 4% ($\sigma_P / P = 0.04$) 
* **Rigidity Range ($P_0$):** 1.0 GV to 1000.0 GV 
* **Mass Reconstruction Bins:** 200 (between 5.0 and 16.0 GeV/c²) 
* **Velocity Windows ($\beta$):** 
  * TOF: $[0.80, 0.85]$ 
  * NaF: $[0.90, 0.95]$ 
  * AGL: $[0.96, 0.99]$

## 1. Basic Diagnostics (1D Distributions)
**Generated by:** `ams_basic_plots.py`

This section validates the foundational kinematic generation and detector smearing logic.

* **Charge, Mass Number & True Mass (`Z_dist.pdf`, `A_dist.pdf`, `m_true_dist.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Events / Bin vs Charge $Z$, Isotopic Mass Number $A$, and True Mass $m_{true}$ [GeV/c²].
  * *Observation:* Confirms the discrete Monte Carlo injection of the cosmic Boron isotopes. The distributions strictly reflect the predefined fixed charge $Z=5$ and the 30% $^{10}B$ / 70% $^{11}B$ absolute abundance ratio prior to any instrumental smearing.
* **Spatial & Angular Incidence (`x_dist.pdf`, `y_dist.pdf`, `cos_theta_dist.pdf`, `theta_dist.pdf`, `phi_dist.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Events / Bin vs spatial coordinates ($x$, $y$) [cm] and incidence angles ($\cos \theta$, $\theta$, $\phi$) [rad].
  * *Observation:* Validates the isotropic generation at the top of the detector, showing a uniform spatial distribution across the initial plane. The zenith angle distributions explicitly demonstrate the geometric acceptance constraints imposed by the TOF trigger, naturally filtering and favoring near-vertical trajectories ($\cos \theta \to 1$).
* **Rigidity, Energy & Weights (`P0_dist.pdf`, `P_dist.pdf`, `e_kn_dist.pdf`, `T0_dist.pdf`, `T_measured_TOF_dist.pdf`, `T_measured_RICH_dist.pdf`, `w_dist.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Events / Bin vs Rigidity ($P_0$, $P$) [GV], Energies ($E_{kn}$, $T_0$, $T_{TOF}$, $T_{RICH}$) [GeV/n, GeV], and Statistical Weight ($w$).
  * *Observation:* Illustrates the modeled primary cosmic-ray kinematics. The histograms confirm the successful application of statistical event weights, correctly transforming the flat log-uniform generation into the expected steeply falling physical power-law spectrum.
* **Velocity Distributions & Residuals (`beta_0_dist.pdf`, `beta_TOF_dist.pdf`, `beta_RICH_dist.pdf`, `res_beta_TOF_dist.pdf`, `res_beta_RICH_dist.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Events / Bin vs Velocity ($\beta$) and Velocity Residuals ($\Delta\beta$).
  * *Observation:* Contrasts the raw kinematics with the distinct instrumental responses. While the TOF provides continuous coverage with visible statistical smearing, the RICH distribution explicitly showcases the hard Cherenkov emission thresholds, inherently restricting valid measurements to the ultra-relativistic regime. The residual plots mathematically confirm the significantly narrower resolution of the RICH ($\sim 10^{-4}$) compared to the TOF ($\sim 10^{-2}$).
* **Reconstructed Masses (`m_TOF_dist.pdf`, `m_RICH_dist.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Events / Bin vs Reconstructed Mass ($m_{TOF}$, $m_{RICH}$) [GeV/c²].
  * *Observation:* Displays the 1D projections of the measured mass. These distributions visually highlight the transition from discrete true masses into broad, overlapping continuous distributions driven entirely by the tracking and velocity resolution limits.

## 2. Advanced Diagnostics (2D & Instrumental Resolution)
**Generated by:** `ams_advanced_plots.py`

This section visualizes the convolutions between the measured phase space and the theoretical Monte Carlo truth.

* **Block A: Kinematics & Generation (`A1_kinematic_spectrum.pdf`, `A2_kinetic_energy_correlation.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Generated Rigidity ($P_0$) unweighted vs weighted; Measured Kinetic Energy vs True Kinetic Energy.
  * *Observation:* Validates the Monte Carlo generation mechanism. The log-uniform generation is successfully reweighted to mirror the steeply falling $P^{-2.7}$ primary cosmic-ray flux. The energy correlation confirms the relativistic reconstruction validity, highlighting the TOF's resolution degradation at high energies as $\beta \to 1$, while the RICH maintains strict linearity above its Cherenkov emission threshold.  
* **Block B: Detector Geometry (`B1_rich_radiator_map.pdf`, `B2_rich_spatial_density.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Extrapolated hit coordinates on the RICH plane ($x_{RICH}$, $y_{RICH}$).
  * *Observation:* Accurately maps the dual-radiator hardware structure, explicitly resolving the central $34.5 \times 34.5$ cm Sodium Fluoride (NaF) square and the outer Aerogel (AGL) ring bounded by a $57.0$ cm radius. The event density plot confirms proper isotropic angular generation and geometric TOF triggering, displaying a uniform spatial distribution of weighted events across the detector's active circular area.
* **Block C: Instrumental Resolution**
  * **Tracker Migration Matrix (`C1_tracker_migration.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* Measured Rigidity ($P$) vs True Rigidity ($P_0$).
    * *Observation:* Visualizes the 4% constant relative uncertainty ($\sigma_P/P = 0.04$) modeled for the tracker. The strict diagonal alignment confirms unbiased Gaussian smearing, with the absolute dispersion naturally broadening at higher rigidities.
  * **Velocity Smearing (`C2_velocity_smearing.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* True Velocity ($\beta_0$), Measured TOF Velocity ($\beta_{TOF}$), and Measured RICH Velocity ($\beta_{RICH}$) vs True Rigidity ($P_0$).
    * *Observation:* Contrasts the distinct responses of the sub-detectors. The TOF provides continuous coverage across all rigidities but with visible statistical broadening. Conversely, the RICH displays a strict Cherenkov kinematic cut-off but achieves a drastically narrower resolution band for ultra-relativistic particles.
  * **Instrumental Beta Correlation (`C3_beta_correlation.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* Measured Velocity $\beta_{RICH}$ vs Measured Velocity $\beta_{TOF}$.
    * *Observation:* Demonstrates the consistency between the two independent velocity measurements. The data perfectly aligns along the $y=x$ diagonal, while the broader horizontal spread explicitly illustrates the lower intrinsic resolution of the TOF compared to the high-precision RICH.
  * **RICH Velocity Resolution (`C4_rich_resolution.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* Relative Velocity Residuals ($\frac{\beta_{RICH} - \beta_0}{\beta_0}$) vs Kinetic Energy per Nucleon ($E_{kn}$).
    * *Observation:* Validates the empirical Cherenkov resolution model for the NaF radiator. The overlaid standard deviation profile ($\sigma$) confirms that the relative error drops rapidly just above the emission threshold and stabilizes into a constant asymptotic regime at higher energies.
  * **RICH Detection Efficiency (`C5_rich_efficiency.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* Detection Efficiency $\epsilon$ vs True Rigidity ($P_0$).
    * *Observation:* Maps the probability of a valid RICH signal for events that successfully triggered the TOF. The distinct step-like jumps precisely reflect the dual-radiator geometry, exposing the separate Cherenkov kinematic thresholds for the NaF and AGL mediums.

* **Block D: Mass Separation**
  * **Banana Curve (`D1_banana_plot.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* Measured Velocity $\beta_{TOF}$ vs Measured Rigidity $P$.
    * *Observation:* Displays the classic relativistic "banana" curve. It highlights how the velocity asymptotically approaches the speed of light ($\beta \to 1$) at higher rigidities, which intrinsically limits the mass separation power of the TOF in that high-energy regime.
  * **Mass vs Rigidity (`D2_mass_separation.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* Reconstructed Mass ($m_{TOF}$ and $m_{RICH}$) vs True Rigidity ($P_0$).
    * *Observation:* Contrasts the mass reconstruction degradation between the sub-detectors. The TOF loses mass resolution at moderate rigidities due to velocity saturation, whereas the RICH maintains a tightly constrained mass band at much higher energies (visible after the Cherenkov emission threshold is reached).
  * **Mass by Radiator (`D3_mass_by_radiator.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* Reconstructed Mass ($m_{RICH}$) separated by radiator type (NaF vs AGL).
    * *Observation:* Compares the integrated 1D mass spectra reconstructed independently by the two RICH radiators. It reflects their relative statistical weights and the overlapping isotopic peaks clustered around 10-11 GeV/c².
  * **Mass Contamination (`D4_mass_contamination.pdf`)**
    * *Variables:* Reconstructed Mass ($m_{TOF}$) segregated by Monte Carlo truth ($^{10}B$ vs $^{11}B$).
    * *Observation:* Explicitly illustrates the significant spatial overlap between the two isotopic distributions. This intrinsic physical contamination visually justifies the absolute necessity of using analytical templates and Maximum Likelihood Estimation (RooFit) to extract accurate isotopic fractions rather than relying on simple event counting.

## 3. Analytical Mass Templates
**Generated by:** `ams_mass_templates.py` & `analyze_templates.py`

Probability Density Functions ($dN/dm$) derived via Bayes' Theorem, factoring in continuous convolutions of the Tracker and Radiator instrumental errors. The analytical transition from the measured phase space to the mass space incorporates the Jacobian transformation $|J| = (\gamma \beta) / Z$ to strictly guarantee probability conservation, forcing the integrated area of each template to exactly 1.

* **TOF Radiator Templates (`plot_templates_TOF_beta080_085.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Normalized Probability ($dN/dm$) vs Reconstructed Mass ($m$).
  * *Observation:* Displays broad theoretical distributions with substantial isotopic overlap. This reflects the intrinsic temporal resolution limits of the scintillators ($\sigma_t$) propagated into the velocity domain for this specific kinematic window ($0.80 < \beta < 0.85$).
* **NaF Radiator Templates (`plot_templates_NaF_beta090_095.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Normalized Probability ($dN/dm$) vs Reconstructed Mass ($m$).
  * *Observation:* Exhibits narrower probability peaks, demonstrating the improved velocity resolution of the Sodium Fluoride radiator ($0.90 < \beta < 0.95$). The reduced isotopic overlap indicates a stronger mass separation capability compared to the TOF.
* **AGL Radiator Templates (`plot_templates_AGL_beta096_099.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Normalized Probability ($dN/dm$) vs Reconstructed Mass ($m$).
  * *Observation:* Shows highly constrained and distinct mass peaks for $^{10}B$ and $^{11}B$. This visually highlights the superior resolving power of the Aerogel radiator in the ultra-relativistic regime ($0.96 < \beta < 0.99$), which effectively minimizes cross-contamination between the adjacent isotopes.

## 4. Isotopic Fraction Fits (RooFit MLE)
**Generated by:** `ams_fraction_fitter.py`

Extended Maximum Likelihood Estimation mapping the simulated flight data against the theoretical mass templates. The RooFit engine natively handles weighted Monte Carlo events using the `SumW2Error(True)` configuration to guarantee rigorous statistical uncertainties during the minimization process.

* **TOF Fit Results (`fit_result_TOF_beta080_085_3.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Events / Bin vs Reconstructed Mass $m$ [GeV/c²].
  * *Observation:* Demonstrates excellent fit convergence over the inherently broad TOF mass distribution. The MLE efficiently resolves the highly overlapping isotopic components, extracting a $^{10}B$ fraction of $38.0 \pm 0.5\%$ in the $0.80 < \beta < 0.85$ velocity window.
* **NaF Fit Results (`fit_result_NaF_beta090_095_3.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Events / Bin vs Reconstructed Mass $m$ [GeV/c²].
  * *Observation:* Shows the total analytical model perfectly matching the simulated data shape for the NaF radiator. The fit yields a $^{10}B$ fraction of $39.2 \pm 1.0\%$ within the $0.90 < \beta < 0.95$ kinematic range.
* **AGL Fit Results (`fit_result_AGL_beta096_099_3.pdf`)**
  * *Variables:* Events / Bin vs Reconstructed Mass $m$ [GeV/c²].
  * *Observation:* Visualizes the precise extraction of isotopic abundances in the ultra-relativistic regime ($0.96 < \beta < 0.99$), extracting a $^{10}B$ fraction of $39.7 \pm 0.3\%$. The deviation from the initial $30\%$ global generation parameter explicitly illustrates the kinematic bias (magnetic suppression effect); since the heavier $^{11}B$ requires higher rigidity to reach the identical $\beta$, the steeply falling primary cosmic flux naturally enriches the fixed velocity bin with the lighter $^{10}B$ isotope.
